\title{Automating Parametric CAD Sketch Synthesis via Sequential Decision Making}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Christopher Luey}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Department of Aeronautics \& Astronautics} \\
\textit{Stanford University}\\
luey@stanford.edu}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We automate early-stage parametric CAD sketching for planar brackets directly from requirement tuples. Leveraging offline trajectories from SketchGraphs, we curate bracket sketches ($\leq$12 primitives, $\leq$3 profile loops), train a behavior cloning (BC) policy with calibrated uncertainty, and fine-tune with conservative Q-learning (CQL) to penalize risky actions. We report curation statistics, calibration metrics, risk-sensitive sweeps, confusion analysis, and qualitative rollouts showing predicted primitives overlayed on ground truth bracket constructions. Results demonstrate that uncertainty-aware offline RL can synthesize bracket sketches with 67\% next-primitive accuracy and well-behaved expected calibration error (ECE 0.13) on held-out requirements, aligning with the proposal objectives.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
CAD automation, offline reinforcement learning, conservative Q-learning, uncertainty calibration, SketchGraphs.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Problem Statement and Motivation}
Modern mechanical design still relies on manual translation of requirement tables into parametric sketches that seed downstream solid modeling. We target planar L/U brackets, aiming to generate sketch construction sequences from structured requirement tuples while respecting design intents (e.g., flange spans, hole patterns). The task is framed as a partially observable MDP where only partial sketch history and requirements are observable; the latent full target sketch is hidden.

\section{Approach Overview}
Our pipeline mirrors the proposal: (1) curate bracket trajectories from SketchGraphs with geometric/loop/primitive filters; (2) learn a BC baseline with calibrated Gaussian heads on primitive parameters; (3) fine-tune via CQL using risk-aware lower-confidence penalties; (4) evaluate with accuracy, risk penalties, ECE, and qualitative rollouts. The action space factors primitive type (Line/Arc/Circle/Point) and continuous parameters (endpoints/centers/radii).

\section{Data Curation (Days 1--4)}
We download \texttt{sg\_t16\_validation.npy} and filter 5{,}000 sequences using proposal heuristics: $\leq$12 primitives, $\leq$3 loops, bracket shape aspect ratio, perpendicular/horizontal/vertical constraints, and at least one circular feature. The retained set is 299 sketches (train 224/val 29/test 46). Primitive and constraint frequencies align with bracket intent (Fig.~\ref{fig:data}).

\section{Modeling and Training (Days 5--9)}
We train a GRU-based state encoder + requirement encoder feeding a policy head (categorical types, Gaussian parameters) with dropout for calibration. BC runs 12 epochs, achieving best val loss 0.49 (Fig.~\ref{fig:training} left). The model vocabulary and padding are derived from curated data metadata to ensure consistency.

\section{Risk-Sensitive Fine-Tuning (Days 10--14)}
CQL initializes from the BC checkpoint and penalizes log-sum-exp vs. data terms with $\alpha=1$. Twenty epochs yield best val loss 0.88 (Fig.~\ref{fig:training} right). Evaluation uses risk-sensitive scoring $\max_a \mathbb{E}[Q]-\lambda\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}}$ and reports risk penalties across $\lambda$.

\section{Analysis and Results}
\subsection{Quantitative Metrics}
On held-out test sketches (46 samples), the CQL policy achieves 67.4\% next-primitive accuracy and ECE 0.132. Risk penalties decrease monotonically with higher $\lambda$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:risk}). Confusion shows most errors between Lines and Arcs, reflecting bracket geometry diversity (Fig.~\ref{fig:confusion}).

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Appropriateness of Approach:} Offline BC + CQL matches the proposal’s uncertainty-aware decision-making framing, avoiding live CAD solver reliance. \textbf{Limitations:} Data is a small curated slice (299 sketches) from the validation shard; richer coverage requires scaling to train shards and relaxing heuristics. Parameter heads assume fixed 6-D layout; richer constraint parameterization is future work. \textbf{Future Work:} Expand dataset, add ensemble/dropout calibration sweeps, and integrate solver-backed validation for geometric satisfaction.

\section{Related Work}
We build on SketchGraphs \cite{SketchGraphs2020} and conservative offline RL \cite{Kumar2020CQL}, adopting uncertainty-aware scoring as discussed by Kochenderfer et al. \cite{Kochenderfer2022}. Prior CAD autocompletion (e.g., Willis et al. \cite{Willis2021Fusion360}) motivates our bracket-specific focus.

\section{Conclusion}
The implemented study fulfills the proposal milestones: curated bracket data, BC baseline with calibration, CQL fine-tuning, and risk-aware evaluation. Quantitative gains and qualitative rollouts show the policy can extend bracket sketches while exposing uncertainty for safer downstream use.
